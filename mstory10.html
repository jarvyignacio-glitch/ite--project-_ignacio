Shadows of a Past Betrayal

— kenshi iwahashi —

Chapter One: The Truth Beneath the Silence

Some betrayals never truly ended. They lingered in the corners of forgotten rooms, beneath the surface of old friendships, and inside the hearts of people who had learned to smile while carrying the weight of a painful secret. In the quiet town of Ravenswood, where ivy covered the walls of abandoned estates and the past seemed to cling to every narrow street, one betrayal had remained hidden for nearly fifteen years. It had destroyed a family, separated lifelong friends, and left behind a mystery that no one had ever dared to solve. Once, the Whitmore family had been among the most respected in Ravenswood. Their estate stood on a hill overlooking the town, surrounded by gardens that bloomed throughout the year and iron gates that rarely remained closed. Their wealth came from generations of business dealings, but their influence extended far beyond money. They supported local schools, funded the town hospital, and maintained the old library that stood near the town square. To outsiders, the Whitmores appeared to possess everything a family could desire. Behind the mansion's elegant walls, however, resentment had been growing for years. The family's downfall began on a stormy evening when Richard Whitmore, the head of the household, was accused of stealing a fortune from his own company. Financial records appeared to prove that millions had disappeared from the business accounts, and several confidential documents suggested that Richard had secretly transferred the money into accounts under his own name. Within days, his reputation collapsed. Business partners withdrew their support, lifelong friends turned against him, and the newspapers filled their pages with accusations that left little room for doubt. Richard insisted that the evidence had been fabricated, but no one believed him. His closest associate, Daniel Hayes, publicly testified against him, claiming that he had discovered the financial irregularities himself. Daniel had been Richard's trusted friend since childhood, a man who had shared his ambitions, celebrated his successes, and stood beside him through every difficulty. His testimony carried enormous weight, and the investigation quickly turned against Richard. Before the case could reach trial, Richard disappeared. His car was found abandoned beside a river several miles outside Ravenswood, its driver's door open and the keys still inside. No body was recovered, and no farewell note was discovered. The authorities eventually presumed that he had died, leaving his wife and young daughter to face the consequences of his disgrace. The Whitmore estate was sold, the family name became a source of gossip, and the people who had once praised Richard learned to speak of him with contempt. Only one person continued to believe in his innocence. His daughter, Isabelle, had been twelve years old when her father vanished. Too young to understand the complicated financial evidence, she remembered something far more important: the fear in her father's eyes during the final weeks before his disappearance. She remembered finding him awake in his study long after midnight, surrounded by documents he repeatedly hid whenever someone approached. Most vividly, she remembered the promise he had made to her the evening before he vanished—that one day, the truth would come to light. Fifteen years later, Isabelle returned to Ravenswood. She had spent most of her adult life away from the town, building a career and attempting to escape the memories that followed her wherever she went. Yet the news of Daniel Hayes's sudden death brought her back to the place she had sworn never to revisit. Daniel, now one of Ravenswood's wealthiest and most influential businessmen, had been found dead inside his locked study. The police described his death as suspicious, though they released few details to the public. Within hours, rumors spread that his death might be connected to an old scandal involving the Whitmore family. Isabelle had received an unexpected package three days before Daniel died. It contained a photograph of her father standing beside Daniel outside the family estate. The picture had been taken shortly before Richard disappeared, and something unusual had been circled in red ink: a third figure standing behind them near the garden gate. The figure's face was partially obscured, but Isabelle recognized the distinctive ring on the person's hand—a silver ring engraved with the symbol of a broken crown. She had seen that ring before. It belonged to someone who had been present during the investigation into her father's disappearance, someone whose testimony had helped destroy the Whitmore family. Inside the package was also a folded letter, its paper brittle with age. The handwriting was unmistakably her father's, though the message seemed to have been written in a hurry. If Daniel is no longer alive, then the person who betrayed us has finally begun to erase the past. Beneath those words was a date, followed by an address that Isabelle recognized as the location of an abandoned railway station on the outskirts of Ravenswood. The station had been closed for decades, its platforms overtaken by weeds and its waiting rooms left to decay. According to local stories, it had once been used to transport goods that never appeared on official shipping records. Isabelle visited the station just before sunset. Beneath a loose floorboard in the old ticket office, she discovered a metal box containing copies of financial documents, photographs, and several letters exchanged between Daniel and an unknown correspondent. The documents suggested that the missing money had never entered Richard's accounts. Instead, it had been transferred through a series of companies connected to someone within the original investigation. Someone had arranged for Richard to take the blame. At the bottom of the box lay a final photograph, its edges darkened by age. It showed three people standing inside the Whitmore study on the night Richard disappeared. Daniel was there, holding a folder of documents. Beside him stood the figure wearing the broken-crown ring. The third person was Isabelle's own mother. For years, Isabelle had believed her mother had suffered alongside her, another victim of the accusations that destroyed their family. She had remembered the quiet grief, the sleepless nights, and the sudden decision to leave Ravenswood after her father's disappearance. Never once had she considered that her mother might have known more than she had admitted. On the back of the photograph was a message that changed everything. The first betrayal was committed by a friend. The second was committed by family. As darkness settled over the abandoned station, Isabelle heard a vehicle stop outside. Headlights swept across the broken windows, briefly illuminating the documents scattered across the floor. Someone stepped out and approached the entrance with measured footsteps. Isabelle gathered the letters and slipped the photograph into her coat. She had come to Ravenswood seeking answers about her father's disappearance, but the evidence suggested that the truth reached far beyond Daniel Hayes and the scandal that had ruined her family. The person responsible was still alive. And that person knew she had returned. When Isabelle finally looked at the photograph again, she noticed a detail she had missed before: beneath her mother's hand, partly hidden by the edge of the table, was a small envelope bearing Isabelle's childhood name. It had been addressed to her fifteen years earlier. Whatever had happened on the night her father disappeared, someone had planned for her to discover the truth one day. The only question was whether the truth had been left for her as a warning—or as an invitation to finish what her father had started.
