The Lighthouse That Hides the Truth

— kenshi iwahashi —

Chapter One: The Light That Never Sleeps

The lighthouse at Greyhaven had been standing for more than a century, watching over the restless sea and guiding ships safely through the darkness. Its white walls were stained by salt and rain, its windows rattled whenever the wind grew fierce, and its ancient lamp continued to sweep across the ocean long after the rest of the town had fallen asleep. To the people of Greyhaven, it was a symbol of safety. To those who remembered what happened twenty years ago, it was a reminder of something they desperately wanted to forget. On the night of October 17, a fishing boat disappeared somewhere beyond the cliffs. Three people were aboard, including the lighthouse keeper, Thomas Whitmore, who had worked at the station for nearly thirty years. The weather had been calm that evening, and the sea showed no signs of the storm that would arrive the following morning. Yet the boat never returned, and despite weeks of searching, not a single body was found. The strangest part was not the disappearance itself. It was the lighthouse. According to the official report, the lamp had gone dark at exactly 11:47 p.m., leaving the surrounding coastline in complete darkness for nearly six hours. Without its guiding light, the fishing boat may have lost its way among the dangerous rocks below the cliffs. The authorities classified the incident as a tragic accident, and the case was eventually closed. But one witness claimed the light had never gone out. That witness was a young woman named Clara Bennett, who lived in a small house overlooking the harbor. She insisted that the lighthouse had continued shining throughout the night, its beam moving steadily across the water as though nothing had happened. She also claimed to have seen a second light flashing from the rocks below the tower shortly before the boat vanished. Three days after giving her statement, Clara disappeared. Her house was found locked from the inside. Her belongings remained untouched, and a cup of tea sat cold on the kitchen table. There were no signs of a struggle, no footprints leading away from the property, and no evidence that she had ever left town. The police found only one unusual object in her bedroom: a photograph of the lighthouse taken on the night of the disappearance. On the back, someone had written a single sentence. The light is a lie. For years, the photograph remained locked inside the evidence room at Greyhaven Police Station. Few people knew it existed, and fewer still were willing to discuss the case. Eventually, Clara's disappearance faded from public memory, becoming another unsolved mystery in a town that had learned to keep its questions to itself. Until the letters began arriving. The first appeared on a cold November morning, addressed to Detective Daniel Reed, who had recently been assigned to review several unresolved cases along the coast. The envelope contained no return address, no fingerprints that could be identified, and no message beyond a short line typed on a plain sheet of paper. Somewhere inside the lighthouse, the truth is still waiting. Attached was a photocopy of the photograph taken twenty years earlier. Daniel studied it beneath the pale light of his office. The image showed the lighthouse standing against a dark sky, its beam stretching across the sea. At first glance, there was nothing unusual about it. The tower appeared intact, the windows were dark, and the surrounding cliffs were barely visible. Then he noticed something near the base of the structure. A figure stood beside the entrance, partially hidden by the shadows. The figure was wearing the same uniform described in the original report as belonging to Thomas Whitmore, the lighthouse keeper who had supposedly vanished at sea. But the photograph had been taken three days after his disappearance. Daniel leaned closer, examining the blurred outline. The image was too damaged to reveal the person's face, but one detail remained clear: the figure was holding a lantern, and its light was directed toward a narrow doorway at the side of the tower. According to the original architectural plans, no such doorway existed. For the first time since taking the case, Daniel felt certain that the official account had concealed something important. He requested access to the original evidence, but several documents were missing. The lighthouse maintenance records had been destroyed in a fire, the original witness statements had been removed from the archive, and the surviving police files contained conflicting dates and unexplained alterations. Someone had worked very carefully to erase the truth. And someone, after twenty years of silence, had decided that the truth needed to be found. That evening, Daniel drove to Greyhaven. The town appeared almost deserted beneath the gray autumn sky. Fishing boats rocked gently in the harbor, shop windows reflected the fading daylight, and the narrow streets leading toward the cliffs were lined with houses whose curtains remained drawn even in the middle of the afternoon. When Daniel asked the locals about the lighthouse, their expressions changed. Some claimed the tower had been abandoned for decades. Others insisted that a caretaker still visited it occasionally, though no one could remember seeing anyone enter or leave. Several residents refused to discuss the disappearance at all, while one elderly shopkeeper quietly advised him to leave the past alone. Daniel had heard similar warnings before. They rarely came from people who had nothing to hide. As night settled over Greyhaven, he followed the winding road toward the cliffs. The lighthouse stood at the edge of the land, surrounded by jagged rocks and dark water. Its lamp swept across the horizon in a slow, deliberate rhythm, illuminating the waves before plunging the coastline back into darkness. He parked beside the old iron gate and stepped into the cold evening air. Something about the place felt wrong. There were fresh tire tracks in the mud, though the road had supposedly been closed for years. The grass near the entrance had been recently trampled, and a narrow line of light escaped from beneath the lighthouse door. Then the lamp stopped moving. For several seconds, the entire coastline fell silent. Daniel looked up at the tower, watching as its great beam remained fixed on a distant point in the ocean. Far below, beyond the reach of the cliffs, another light appeared between the waves. Once. Twice. Then a third time. He remembered Clara Bennett's statement. A second light flashing from the rocks below the tower shortly before the boat vanished. Daniel reached for his phone, but before he could make a call, the lighthouse lamp suddenly went dark. In the silence that followed, he heard something from inside the tower. Three slow knocks. A pause. Then three more. He approached the door, his footsteps echoing against the stone path. The handle turned easily beneath his hand, revealing a narrow corridor that smelled of damp wood and seawater. Dust covered the floor, but near the staircase were fresh footprints leading upward. At the bottom of the stairs lay a small envelope. His name was written across the front. Inside was a single photograph, its edges blackened as though it had survived a fire. It showed the missing fishing boat resting beside a hidden dock beneath the cliffs. Three figures stood on the deck, their faces turned toward the camera. One of them was Thomas Whitmore. Another was Clara Bennett. The third figure wore a police uniform. Daniel recognized the face immediately. It belonged to the chief inspector who had officially closed the case twenty years earlier. Beneath the photograph was a final message, written in dark ink. You were never supposed to find the door. A sudden noise came from the staircase above him. Daniel looked up. Someone was standing at the top of the stairs, hidden in the darkness. And in their hand was a lantern glowing with the same pale light he had seen in the old photograph. The lighthouse had been silent for twenty years. Now, for the first time, someone inside it was waiting for him. And Daniel was beginning to understand that the greatest mystery was not why three people had disappeared on that October night. It was why someone had gone to such extraordinary lengths to make the entire town believe they were dead. Because the light had never gone out. Someone had simply been controlling where it shone. And whatever lay hidden beneath the lighthouse was still waiting to be discovered.
