A Promise Beneath the Cherry Blossoms

— kenshi iwahashi —

Chapter One: The Day We Promised Forever

There was something magical about cherry blossoms. Perhaps it was the way their soft pink petals danced in the wind, or how they made even the simplest moments feel like something worth remembering. To everyone else, they were just flowers that bloomed for a little while before falling to the ground. But to me, they were a reminder of a promise I had made long ago—a promise I thought time could never erase. I was seventeen when I first met him. It was a quiet spring afternoon, and the school courtyard was covered in pale pink petals. I had always loved that place because it felt like a little world of its own, far away from the noise and expectations of everyone else. Whenever life became too overwhelming, I would sit beneath the oldest cherry blossom tree and write in my journal. That afternoon, however, I wasn't alone. A boy stood beneath the tree, holding a small notebook in one hand while staring up at the falling petals. His dark hair moved gently with the breeze, and there was something strangely familiar about the sadness in his eyes. I probably would have walked away if he hadn't spoken first. “Do you come here often?” I looked at him, surprised. “Only when I need somewhere quiet.” He smiled faintly. “Then I guess we have something in common.” It wasn't love at first sight. At least, that's what I told myself back then. He was simply a stranger who happened to share my favorite place, someone whose name I didn't even know. But sometimes, the most important people in our lives arrive quietly, without warning, and change everything before we even realize it. His name was Haruto. Over the next few weeks, we began spending our afternoons beneath that same cherry blossom tree. We talked about our dreams, our fears, and the futures we imagined for ourselves. He wanted to become a photographer, someone who could capture moments before they disappeared. I wanted to become a writer, someone who could turn ordinary feelings into stories that would last forever. He often teased me about how seriously I took my writing, and I would laugh whenever he showed me photographs of flowers, sunsets, and little things most people never noticed. Somewhere between those afternoons and the countless conversations that followed, he became more than just a boy I met beneath a tree. He became the person I looked for in every crowd. The voice I wanted to hear after a difficult day. And the reason I started believing that some people were worth waiting for. But neither of us knew that the following spring would bring a goodbye neither of us was prepared for. One afternoon, as the cherry blossoms began to fall around us, Haruto handed me a photograph. It was a picture of the two of us sitting beneath our tree, laughing at something I could no longer remember. On the back, he had written a single sentence. “No matter where life takes us, meet me here when the cherry blossoms bloom again.” I read it twice before looking up at him. “Why are you making it sound like you're going somewhere?” For a moment, he said nothing. Then he looked away, his fingers tightening around the strap of his camera. “There are things I need to do,” he finally whispered. “Things I can't explain yet.” I wanted to ask him a thousand questions, but the fear in his voice stopped me. Instead, I held out my little finger. “Then promise me you'll come back.” He looked at my hand, then at me. A small smile appeared on his face as he linked his finger with mine. “I promise.” Beneath the falling cherry blossoms, we made a promise that felt strong enough to last forever. Neither of us understood how difficult forever could be. And neither of us knew that, sometimes, the hardest promises to keep are the ones made by people who truly want to stay. Because the following morning, Haruto was gone. No goodbye. No explanation. Not even a message. Only a photograph, a promise, and a cherry blossom tree that continued to bloom as if nothing had happened. I waited for him that spring. Then the next. And every year afterward, whenever the pink petals began to fall, I returned to the place where our story had started. I told myself I was only remembering. But deep down, I was still waiting. Waiting for the boy who promised to come back. Waiting for the answer to a goodbye I never received. And waiting to discover whether a promise made beneath the cherry blossoms could survive the passage of time. Because no matter how many years passed, I could never forget the words he had written on the back of that photograph. “No matter where life takes us, meet me here when the cherry blossoms bloom again.” And every spring, I did.
