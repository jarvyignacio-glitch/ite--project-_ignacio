Secrets Buried in the Garden

— kenshi iwahashi —

Chapter One: The Flowers That Hid the Truth

Every garden has a story, but not every story is meant to be told. Behind the tall iron gates of an old estate stood a garden that had remained untouched for nearly twenty years. Its winding stone paths were covered in moss, its rose bushes had grown wild, and ivy climbed the cracked walls as though nature itself were trying to conceal what lay beneath. To strangers, it was nothing more than a forgotten piece of land belonging to a family that had long since disappeared from public life. But to those who knew its history, the garden was something else entirely. It was a place of unanswered questions, whispered accusations, and a disappearance that had never been explained. The town of Blackwood had always been known for its quiet streets and beautiful old houses, where everyone seemed to know everyone else's business. Yet beneath its peaceful appearance lay years of carefully guarded secrets. Families protected their reputations, neighbors avoided uncomfortable questions, and certain names were spoken only in whispers. Among those names was the Ashford family. Once one of the wealthiest and most respected families in town, the Ashfords had lived in a magnificent manor at the edge of Blackwood for generations. Their estate was famous for its elaborate gardens, where rare flowers bloomed throughout the year and a grand fountain stood at the center of a maze of hedges. Then, one autumn evening, everything changed. Eleanor Ashford, the youngest daughter of the family, vanished without a trace. She was seventeen years old. According to the official report, Eleanor had left the estate sometime after dinner and never returned. Search parties combed the surrounding woods, police questioned the household staff, and newspapers published her photograph for weeks. Despite the attention, no evidence was ever found to explain what had happened to her. No body was discovered. No ransom was demanded. No reliable witness came forward. Eventually, the investigation went cold, and the town slowly returned to its ordinary routines. The Ashford family withdrew from public life, the manor was abandoned, and the garden that Eleanor had once loved became overgrown and forgotten. But the mystery never truly disappeared. For twenty years, rumors continued to circulate. Some claimed Eleanor had run away to escape her family. Others believed she had discovered something dangerous hidden within the estate. A few insisted that the truth had been buried long before the night she vanished. Most people dismissed these stories as nothing more than gossip. I might have done the same if I had not received the letter. It arrived on a rainy Thursday morning, delivered in a plain envelope with no return address. Inside was a single sheet of paper, yellowed at the edges and marked with a handwritten message that sent a chill through me. Some secrets grow beneath the flowers. If you want to know what happened to Eleanor Ashford, look where the white roses bloom. There was no signature. No explanation. Only a small photograph tucked beneath the paper. It showed a young woman standing in the Ashford garden, her face turned toward the camera. Behind her, the manor's windows reflected the afternoon sunlight, and a white rose rested between her fingers. On the back of the photograph were three words. She never left. I read them again and again, trying to convince myself that there had to be a reasonable explanation. Someone was playing a cruel joke, perhaps, or attempting to revive a story that should have remained in the past. Yet something about the photograph unsettled me. The young woman was unmistakably Eleanor Ashford, whose face I recognized from the old newspaper clippings I had studied while researching Blackwood's most infamous unsolved mysteries. But it was not her expression that caught my attention. It was the garden behind her. Near the far wall, partially hidden by climbing ivy, stood a stone statue of an angel. Its right hand was raised toward the sky, while its left pointed toward a narrow path leading behind the rose bushes. I had seen that statue before. It appeared in several photographs taken shortly before Eleanor's disappearance. In every image, the angel stood in the same position, watching over the garden like a silent guardian. But in the photograph I held, something was different. At the base of the statue, the soil appeared freshly disturbed. Someone had been digging. And judging by the date printed in the corner, the photograph had been taken only three days before Eleanor vanished. I spent the rest of the morning searching through every article, photograph, and document I could find about the Ashford case. The more I uncovered, the less the official account made sense. Witness statements contradicted one another. Several pages from the original investigation were missing, and the family's former groundskeeper had left town without providing a forwarding address. Even stranger, every reference to the garden had disappeared from the final police report. It was as though someone had deliberately removed the one place where the truth might still be waiting. By the time evening arrived, my curiosity had become something far more difficult to ignore. I knew I should have taken the letter to the authorities. I knew that investigating an old disappearance without understanding its history could lead me into dangerous territory. But the words on the photograph continued to haunt me. She never left. If Eleanor had never left the estate, then where had she gone? And if someone knew the truth, why had they waited twenty years to reveal even the smallest piece of it? Outside my window, rain fell steadily against the glass. I placed the photograph on my desk and studied the young woman's face one final time. There was something strangely familiar about her eyes, though I could not explain why. For a moment, I imagined her standing alone in that garden, surrounded by white roses and towering hedges, knowing something terrible was about to happen. Then I noticed a detail I had overlooked. Eleanor was wearing a small silver locket around her neck. The same locket appeared in another photograph, taken nearly a year after her disappearance. It was worn by someone else. Someone whose identity had never been mentioned in any account of the case. And beneath that photograph, written in the same handwriting as the mysterious letter, were four words that changed everything. Trust no one in Blackwood. That night, I made a decision I would later wish I had thought through more carefully. The following morning, I would go to the Ashford estate. I would find the white roses, examine the ground beneath them, and discover why someone had gone to such lengths to hide the truth. I believed I was investigating a disappearance. I had no idea that the garden held a secret much darker than Eleanor's fate—a secret that connected my own family to the Ashfords, and a truth that someone had spent twenty years trying to keep buried. Because the most dangerous secrets are not always the ones hidden underground. Sometimes, they are the ones living quietly among us, waiting for the right moment to bloom. And in Blackwood, the flowers were beginning to bloom again.
