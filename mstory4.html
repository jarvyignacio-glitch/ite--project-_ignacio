The Silent Witness in Room 9

— kenshi iwahashi —

Chapter One: The Room That Remembered

At the end of the ninth corridor of an aging hotel stood Room 9, a place surrounded by rumors, unanswered questions, and a silence that seemed too heavy to be ordinary. Its wooden door had been repainted countless times, yet the number engraved upon it remained dark and weathered, as though the years had failed to touch it. Guests rarely requested the room, and those who were assigned to it often asked to be moved before morning. Some complained of restless nights, while others spoke of strange shadows appearing beneath the door when the hallway lights flickered. The hotel had once been a place of elegance, filled with travelers, musicians, and wealthy visitors seeking refuge from the busy world outside. But everything changed on a rainy evening fifteen years earlier, when a young woman was discovered dead inside Room 9. The windows had been locked from the inside, the curtains were drawn, and the only key was found on the bedside table. No signs of forced entry were discovered, and no one could explain how the woman had died. The investigation continued for months before the case was quietly closed, leaving behind a mystery that refused to fade. The official records described the death as an unfortunate accident, but the hotel staff knew there had been more to the story. On the night of the incident, several guests had reported hearing footsteps outside the room, followed by the sound of something heavy striking the floor. A housekeeper claimed she had seen a figure standing at the end of the corridor shortly before midnight, though she could not remember its face. The night manager, who had been responsible for the guest registry, resigned without explanation just days after the investigation ended. Years passed, and the hotel changed owners twice. The old staff left, the furniture was replaced, and the walls were decorated with fresh paintings intended to hide the building's troubled past. Yet Room 9 remained untouched. Its bed was never moved, its mirror was never replaced, and the small clock above the dresser continued to show the same time: 11:47. No one remembered who had stopped it, and no repairman could explain why it refused to work again. Then, one quiet evening, a sealed envelope appeared beneath the hotel's front door. It contained no return address, no signature, and only a single photograph. The image showed the corridor outside Room 9 on the night of the woman's death. At first glance, nothing seemed unusual. The hallway was empty, the lamps glowed softly, and the door stood closed. But in the reflection of the mirror opposite the room, a second figure could be seen standing inside. The police had never mentioned a second person in their reports. Beneath the photograph was a short, handwritten message stating that the wrong person had been blamed, and that the real killer had never left the hotel. By morning, the envelope had disappeared from the reception desk, and the security footage from the previous night had been erased. Only the photograph remained, hidden inside the hotel's oldest record book, beside a guest register containing one name that had been crossed out so violently the paper had nearly torn. That name belonged to someone who had checked into Room 9 on the night of the murder. And according to the hotel's records, that person had never checked out.
