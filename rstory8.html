Love in Every Stolen Moment

— kenshi iwahashi —

Chapter One: The Moments Between Us

Some of the most beautiful moments in life are the ones we never plan to remember. They happen in the quiet spaces between ordinary days, hidden in fleeting glances, unfinished conversations, and the seconds that seem insignificant until we realize how much they mean to us. They are the moments we wish we could keep forever, even when we know they were never meant to last. I never understood that kind of love until I met someone who made time feel different. Before then, my life had followed a familiar rhythm. Each day passed much like the one before it, filled with routines, responsibilities, and the quiet expectation that everything would eventually fall into place. I believed that happiness was something we found by following the right path, making the right decisions, and avoiding the mistakes that could leave us with regrets. Love, to me, was supposed to be simple. It was supposed to arrive at the right time, in the right circumstances, without leaving us questioning what we meant to one another. I thought that if two people cared enough, there would always be a way to make things work. But life rarely follows the stories we imagine for ourselves. Sometimes, the people who change us the most enter our lives at the most inconvenient times. Sometimes, the feelings we try to ignore become the ones we carry the longest. And sometimes, the moments we treasure most are the ones we can never fully explain to anyone else. That was how it began for us. There was no extraordinary first meeting, no sudden realization that my life was about to change. Instead, it happened gradually, through little things I barely noticed at first. A familiar presence that made difficult days easier. A quiet understanding that grew without either of us asking for it. A happiness that lingered long after we had gone our separate ways. Before I knew it, ordinary moments had become something I looked forward to. The smallest encounters began to mean more than they should have. A few minutes together could brighten an entire afternoon, while a brief glance could stay in my thoughts long after the day had ended. Even silence became something precious when it was shared with the right person. It was strange how someone could become so important without ever asking for a place in your life. One moment, they were simply another person among many. The next, you found yourself noticing their absence, remembering the smallest things about them, and wishing that time would move a little more slowly whenever they were near. Perhaps that was the danger of stolen moments. They made you want more. More time, more memories, more opportunities to discover the person hidden beneath the smiles and familiar gestures. They made you believe that a few extra minutes could somehow make up for all the things left unsaid. And so, I learned to treasure what little time we had. I learned that affection could live in the smallest gestures, that happiness did not always need a reason, and that some feelings grew strongest in the spaces where words were never spoken. I began to understand that love was not always found in grand declarations or promises made beneath the stars. Sometimes, it existed in the moments nobody else noticed. But beautiful moments can also carry the weight of things left unresolved. There were questions I was afraid to ask and feelings I could not bring myself to name. There were times when I wondered whether what we shared meant as much to the other person as it did to me. I feared that giving those feelings a name might change everything, turning something beautiful into something uncertain. So I held on to the moments instead. I told myself that it was enough to be there, enough to share a little laughter, enough to leave with another memory worth keeping. I convinced myself that some connections were better left undefined, protected from the expectations that came with wanting something more. Yet the heart has its own way of revealing the truth. No matter how carefully we hide our feelings, they find their way into the things we do, the people we remember, and the moments we wish would never end. Eventually, even the smallest part of us begins to hope that the person standing beside us might be wishing for the same things. Looking back, I realize that I was never afraid of loving someone. I was afraid of discovering that all those stolen moments meant something different to them. Because sometimes, the hardest part of falling in love is not the uncertainty of how it begins, but the possibility that it might never become what we secretly hope it will be. Still, if I could return to those days, I would not ask for grander gestures or longer promises. I would simply ask for a little more time—to notice the beauty of what we had, to remember every ordinary detail, and to appreciate the moments before they became memories. Because not every love story begins with certainty. Some begin with a feeling neither person knows how to explain, growing quietly through moments borrowed from the rest of the world. And sometimes, those stolen moments become the memories we carry for the rest of our lives. I did not know where our story would lead, or whether the feelings growing between us would ever find the courage to become something more. All I knew was that whenever we were together, the world seemed to slow down just enough for me to believe that, for a little while, nothing else mattered. Perhaps that was what made it beautiful. Perhaps that was what made it frightening. Or perhaps that was simply what it meant to fall in love—one fleeting moment at a time. And if I had known how much those moments would eventually mean to me, I would have held on to every single one a little longer.
