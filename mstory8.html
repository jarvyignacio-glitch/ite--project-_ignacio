What the River Saw That Night

— kenshi iwahashi —

Chapter One: The Secret Beneath the Water

The river that ran through the town of Willow Creek had witnessed generations come and go. It flowed beneath the old stone bridge, curved around abandoned farmlands, and disappeared into a dense forest where the trees grew so close together that sunlight rarely touched the ground. During the day, its surface shimmered beneath the golden light, appearing peaceful enough to hide the worries of those who lived nearby. But when night fell, the river transformed into something unfamiliar. Its waters darkened, its currents grew strangely restless, and the mist that rose from its surface carried an unsettling silence through the surrounding fields. For as long as anyone could remember, the people of Willow Creek had treated the river with a mixture of respect and fear. Children were warned not to swim beyond the shallow banks, fishermen avoided certain stretches after sunset, and older residents refused to cross the stone bridge whenever the moon disappeared behind the clouds. Their caution was often dismissed as superstition, but those who had lived in the town long enough understood that some stories were repeated not because they were entertaining, but because someone had once learned the truth the hard way. Twenty years earlier, a young woman named Evelyn Moore had disappeared beside the river on a cold November night. She had been seen leaving a gathering at the old mill, walking alone toward the bridge with a small leather bag held tightly against her chest. By morning, her belongings had been discovered near the water's edge, but Evelyn herself was nowhere to be found. The authorities searched the river for days, dragging its muddy depths and questioning everyone who had attended the gathering. Despite their efforts, no body was recovered, and no one was ever charged with a crime. The case eventually went cold. Newspapers stopped printing her photograph, her family left Willow Creek, and the townspeople learned to speak of Evelyn in the past tense. The official explanation suggested that she had fallen into the river and been carried away by the current. Yet the theory never fully explained the evidence left behind. Her shoes had been found neatly placed beside the bridge, her bag had been closed and locked, and the muddy ground showed no clear signs of a struggle. Most unsettling was the testimony of a witness who claimed to have seen someone standing on the opposite riverbank that night. The figure had remained motionless beneath the trees, watching Evelyn until she disappeared from view. When investigators attempted to identify the witness, they discovered that the name provided during the original inquiry belonged to someone who had died several months before the disappearance. The report was quietly amended, and the testimony vanished from the public record. Years passed, and the river continued to flow as though nothing had happened. New families settled in Willow Creek, the old mill fell into ruin, and the stone bridge became a popular place for evening walks. Yet every November, on the anniversary of Evelyn's disappearance, residents reported seeing a pale light moving beneath the water. Some described it as a lantern drifting against the current, while others claimed it resembled a face appearing briefly beneath the surface before dissolving into darkness. Then, one autumn morning, the river gave something back. Following several days of heavy rain, the water level dropped unexpectedly, exposing a narrow strip of muddy ground beneath the old bridge. Among the stones and tangled roots lay a small silver locket, its surface darkened by years underwater. Inside was a faded photograph of Evelyn Moore, smiling beside a man whose face had been scratched away. Engraved on the back of the locket were three words: He knows everything. The discovery reopened questions that the town had spent two decades trying to forget. The locket was identified as Evelyn's by an elderly shopkeeper who had repaired its broken clasp shortly before her disappearance. But when the police attempted to locate the original case files, several important documents were missing, including the witness statements from the night she vanished. Someone had taken them. That same evening, a retired detective named Thomas Reed received an anonymous package at his home. Inside was a photograph of the stone bridge taken on the night Evelyn disappeared. The image was grainy and blurred, but three figures could be distinguished beneath the streetlamp: Evelyn, a man standing behind her, and a third person watching from the trees. On the back of the photograph, a sentence had been written in ink that had begun to fade. The river did not take her. Thomas had spent twenty years convincing himself that Evelyn's disappearance was a tragedy without a solution. He had led the original investigation, questioned the witnesses, and signed the final report declaring the case closed. Yet he remembered one detail that had never appeared in the official records: on the morning after Evelyn vanished, he had discovered a trail of wet footprints leading away from the river and toward the home of one of Willow Creek's most respected residents. He had followed the trail. And then, for reasons he had never explained, he had stopped investigating. Now, as darkness settled over the town, Thomas returned to the old bridge with the photograph hidden inside his coat. The river moved quietly beneath him, its surface reflecting the distant lights of the houses along the bank. For a moment, everything seemed unchanged, as though the years had passed without leaving a mark. Then he noticed a shape beneath the water. It stood directly below the bridge, facing upward, its pale hands pressed against the underside of the surface. The current rushed around it without moving its body, and its face remained hidden beneath a curtain of dark hair. Thomas stepped back, his breath caught in his throat. From somewhere beneath the water came three slow knocks. The same three knocks he had heard on the night Evelyn disappeared. And as the river began to rise around the bridge, Thomas finally understood that the truth had never been lost.
