Letters never sent to you

— kenshi iwahashi —

Chapter One: If You Ever Read This

here are so many things I wish I could tell you. Things I should have said when you were still here, words I swallowed because I was afraid, and feelings I kept hidden behind a smile whenever you looked my way. Sometimes, I wonder what would have happened if I had been brave enough to tell you the truth. Would things have been different? Would you have stayed? Or would we have ended the same way, with two hearts full of words and no courage to speak them? I guess I'll never know. So instead of telling you everything, I started writing. I wrote about the days I missed you the most, the little things that reminded me of you, and the nights when your name crossed my mind even when I tried so hard to forget it. I wrote about the conversations we never finished and the promises that slowly became memories. I wrote about the things I wanted to say when we were together but couldn't, and the things I wished I could take back after we fell apart. Every letter began with your name. And every letter ended the same way—with words I could never bring myself to send. Maybe I was afraid of what you would think. Maybe I was afraid you wouldn't care anymore. Or maybe, deep down, I knew that sending those letters would mean admitting that a part of me was still waiting for you. Waiting for an apology. Waiting for an explanation. Waiting for a sign that what we had once shared meant something to you, too. But the days turned into months, and the months became years. Life continued, even when my heart seemed determined to stay in the past. I learned how to laugh without looking for you in a crowded room. I learned how to spend my days without wondering what you were doing. I even learned how to tell people that I was fine without feeling like I was lying every time. Still, I never threw the letters away. They remained tucked inside an old box beneath my bed, tied together with a faded ribbon. Sometimes, I would take them out and read them again, wondering how someone could leave such a lasting mark on a life they were no longer part of. Perhaps that was the hardest thing about losing you. You became a stranger I knew by heart. And if you ever asked me whether I regretted loving you, I would probably smile and tell you no. Because despite the tears, the sleepless nights, and all the things we failed to become, there was a time when loving you was the most beautiful thing I had ever known. But if you asked me whether I regretted never telling you how much you meant to me, I wouldn't know what to say. Because the truth is, there are words that become heavier the longer we keep them to ourselves. And these letters? They carry every single one of them. I don't know if you'll ever read them. I don't even know if I want you to. Maybe they're better left where they are, hidden away with the memories of who we used to be. But tonight, for the first time in a long while, I'm going to write another one. Not because I expect you to come back. Not because I'm hoping you'll finally understand. But because there are some goodbyes that take more than silence to say. And maybe, just maybe, this will be the last letter I ever write to you. Dear You, I don't know where life has taken you, or whether you ever think about me the way I still think about you. But if these words ever find their way to you, I hope you know that somewhere in this world, there was someone who loved you quietly, deeply, and more than they ever knew how to say. That someone was me. And this is the story of everything I never sent you
