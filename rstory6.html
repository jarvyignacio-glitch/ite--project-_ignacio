The Cottage Where Love Waits

— kenshi iwahashi —

Chapter One: A Place Called Home

Some places hold more than memories. They hold the laughter of people who once lived within their walls, the warmth of afternoons that seemed to last forever, and the quiet echoes of love that never truly learned how to leave. For as long as I could remember, I believed that home was a place you returned to when the world became too difficult to face. Somewhere familiar, somewhere safe, somewhere that welcomed you without asking why you had been gone for so long. But growing up taught me that not every home stays the same, and not every place we leave behind is ready to welcome us back. I discovered this truth the summer I returned to the little cottage by the lake. Hidden among tall trees and winding country roads, the cottage stood quietly beside the water, its white wooden walls weathered by years of rain and sunlight. Wildflowers grew along the narrow path leading to its front door, and an old wooden swing rested beneath the branches of a tree that had stood there long before I was born. Every morning, sunlight slipped through the curtains, filling the rooms with a golden warmth that made the world outside seem impossibly far away. It was a place untouched by the hurried rhythm of the city, where time moved gently and even silence seemed to have a language of its own. As a child, I had spent countless summers there, believing those days would never end. I remembered running barefoot across the grass, collecting little stones along the lakeshore, and watching the sunset paint the water in shades of amber and rose. I remembered the smell of freshly baked bread drifting from the kitchen and the sound of rain tapping against the windows on quiet afternoons. Most of all, I remembered the feeling of belonging. Back then, the cottage had seemed like a world where nothing bad could ever happen. It was where happiness came easily, where tomorrow was something to look forward to, and where the people we loved always seemed to be within reach. But time has a way of changing even the places we believe will remain forever. Years passed, childhood faded into distant memories, and life carried me far away from the lake and the little cottage that had once meant everything to me. Other responsibilities took its place, along with disappointments I never spoke about and dreams that slowly became harder to recognize. I learned how to keep moving forward, even when a part of me longed for the simplicity of the life I had left behind. Eventually, the cottage became nothing more than a place I remembered whenever the world felt too heavy. Until the day I returned. I told myself I had come back for practical reasons, to settle unfinished matters and spend a little time away from the life I had built elsewhere. I convinced myself that the cottage was simply an old property filled with forgotten belongings, nothing more than another chapter of my past waiting to be closed. Yet standing before its familiar front door, I felt something stir inside me. It was the strange ache of remembering a life that no longer existed, mixed with the quiet hope that perhaps not everything had changed. The wooden steps still creaked beneath my feet. The lake still reflected the colors of the sky. And the old swing still moved gently whenever the wind passed through the trees. For the first time in years, I allowed myself to wonder whether some things could remain beautiful despite the passage of time. I did not know that the cottage still held secrets I had never discovered, or that the memories waiting inside its walls would lead me toward someone I had never expected to find again. I did not know that the past I had tried so hard to leave behind was quietly preparing to meet me at the very place where everything had once begun. And I certainly did not know that love, even after years of silence and distance, could still be waiting in the most unexpected places. Perhaps that was the mystery of the cottage. It never asked anyone to stay, yet it always seemed to leave the door open. It never promised that people would return, yet it held their memories as if it believed they might someday find their way home. Maybe some places remember us better than we remember ourselves. Maybe some goodbyes are not endings, but pauses in stories that have yet to be finished. And maybe love is not always something we must chase across the world. Sometimes, it waits quietly in a place we once knew by heart, surrounded by everything we thought we had forgotten. That summer, I returned to the cottage believing I was there to close a door on my past. I had no idea that behind that door, a new beginning was waiting. And for the first time in a long while, I began to believe that perhaps home was not simply where I had come from. Perhaps it was where my heart had been waiting to return.
