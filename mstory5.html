Vanishing at Midnight Hollow

— kenshi iwahashi —

Chapter One: The Town That Disappeared

There were places in the world that seemed to exist outside the reach of time, hidden behind mountains, swallowed by forests, or forgotten by the people who once called them home. Midnight Hollow was one of those places. Tucked between a stretch of dark pine woods and a winding road that rarely appeared on modern maps, the small town had once been known for its peaceful streets, old-fashioned houses, and a church whose bell could be heard across the valley. But beneath its quiet appearance lay a history that no resident dared to discuss, a history marked by unexplained disappearances and a night that had never truly ended. Twenty-five years earlier, the town had vanished from the world in a single evening. Nearly every resident disappeared without warning, leaving behind warm meals on kitchen tables, unfinished letters beside open windows, and clocks frozen at exactly midnight. There were no signs of a struggle, no abandoned vehicles along the roads, and no evidence that the townspeople had fled. It was as though an invisible hand had reached into Midnight Hollow and erased every living soul, leaving the buildings and belongings untouched. Search parties arrived in the days that followed, drawn by reports from neighboring villages that the town's lights had gone out all at once. They found empty homes, silent shops, and a schoolhouse where notebooks remained open on desks. In the town square, a fountain continued to spill water into its stone basin, despite the fact that the entire electrical system had mysteriously failed. The church doors were locked from the inside, and its bell tower showed no signs of damage. Most unsettling of all, every clock in the town had stopped at midnight, down to the smallest watch left beside a child's bed. The authorities eventually declared Midnight Hollow uninhabitable and closed the roads leading into it. Official reports blamed a landslide and a series of unexplained structural failures, but no evidence of either disaster was ever presented to the public. The case slowly faded from newspapers and television broadcasts, becoming nothing more than an unsettling local legend. Over time, the town was removed from maps, its name disappeared from travel guides, and the few people who remembered its existence learned to keep their memories to themselves. Yet Midnight Hollow had never been entirely forgotten. Every few years, someone claimed to have seen lights glowing between the trees, despite the absence of electricity. Travelers spoke of hearing a church bell ring in the distance when no church stood nearby. Some reported finding unfamiliar photographs in their belongings after passing through the surrounding forest, each image showing the same empty town beneath a moonless sky. In the background of every photograph, the clock tower displayed the same impossible time. Then, on the twenty-fifth anniversary of the disappearance, a young woman named Elena received a letter addressed in handwriting she had not seen since childhood. The envelope contained no return address, no postage mark, and no explanation for how it had reached her apartment. Inside was a faded photograph of Midnight Hollow, taken from the entrance of the town. Behind the familiar rows of abandoned houses, a single figure stood beneath the clock tower, its face hidden by shadow. On the back of the photograph were seven words written in dark ink: Midnight is coming. Come back before it does. Elena had spent her entire life believing that her memories of Midnight Hollow were fragments of a childhood dream. She remembered a narrow street lined with white houses, a garden filled with dying roses, and a woman whose face she could never clearly picture. Most of all, she remembered the sound of a clock striking twelve and the sudden, terrible silence that followed. She had been only six years old when her family disappeared. No one had ever explained how Elena had survived, or how she had been found alone on a road miles from the town. The records listed her parents as missing, her childhood home as abandoned, and her past as a collection of unanswered questions. She had learned to live without the truth, convincing herself that some mysteries were better left untouched. But the letter changed everything. That night, as the hands of her bedroom clock approached midnight, every light in her apartment flickered and went dark. Somewhere beyond the city, a church bell rang twelve times, though no church nearby had ever possessed a bell. When the final chime faded, Elena discovered a thin layer of frost spreading across the photograph in her hands. The figure beneath the clock tower was no longer standing in the distance. It was closer now, directly outside the entrance to the town. And beneath its shadowed face, Elena recognized the coat her father had been wearing on the night he vanished. For the first time in twenty-five years, Midnight Hollow had sent a message to the world. And this time, it wanted someone to answer.
