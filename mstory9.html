The Key to the Forgotten Crime

— kenshi iwahashi —

Chapter One: The Secret Behind the Locked Door

In the oldest part of Blackwood, where narrow streets twisted between abandoned buildings and the fog settled heavily over the rooftops, stood a mansion that had remained locked for nearly thirty years. Its windows were covered with dust, its iron gates were consumed by rust, and the garden that once displayed carefully trimmed hedges had become a wilderness of thorny vines and dead flowers. The people of the town called it the Ashford House, a name that had once represented wealth, influence, and respect. Now, it was remembered for a crime so disturbing that even mentioning it could silence an entire room. On a winter night three decades earlier, the owner of the mansion, wealthy businessman Edward Ashford, had been found dead inside his private study. The door had been locked from the inside, the windows were sealed, and the only key was missing. There were no signs of forced entry, no clear evidence of a struggle, and no witnesses who could explain what had happened during the hours before his death. The police questioned the household staff, examined the property, and searched every corner of the estate, but the investigation produced more questions than answers. Edward's death was eventually ruled a homicide, though no suspect was ever convicted. Several members of the Ashford family had been present at the mansion that evening, each with a possible motive and each offering a different account of the final hours before the crime. His eldest son stood to inherit the family fortune, his younger daughter had recently been removed from his will, and his business partner had been involved in a bitter financial dispute with him. Even the housekeeper, who had served the family for more than twenty years, gave a statement that contradicted the evidence found in the study. Within a year, the Ashford family had scattered. The mansion was abandoned, the business collapsed, and the investigation was quietly closed. The missing key was never recovered, and the final police report disappeared from the town's courthouse archives. As time passed, the murder became a half-forgotten story, repeated by locals whenever someone dared to approach the old estate after dark. But the crime had never truly been forgotten. Thirty years later, a young investigative journalist named Claire Bennett returned to Blackwood after receiving an unexpected inheritance from a distant relative. Among the legal documents was a small wooden box containing several yellowed newspaper clippings, a faded family photograph, and a brass key wrapped in a piece of black cloth. The key was unusually heavy, its surface marked with a symbol resembling an eye surrounded by three narrow lines. Beneath the key lay a handwritten note. The truth is not buried. It is locked away. Claire initially assumed the box contained nothing more than family curiosities. However, when she examined the newspaper clippings, she discovered that every article concerned Edward Ashford's murder. Some described the original investigation, while others mentioned witnesses who had mysteriously changed their statements or vanished from Blackwood shortly after being questioned. One clipping had been cut from a newspaper published only days after the crime, and a sentence had been circled in faded red ink. The police had discovered a second room hidden behind the study's bookshelves. The room had never been mentioned in any later report. Intrigued, Claire began searching the town's historical records. She found references to a renovation carried out at the mansion shortly before Edward's death, but the building plans had been removed from the public archive. The architect responsible for the work had died under unexplained circumstances, and the contractor had left Blackwood without collecting his final payment. More strangely, the mansion's original floor plan showed a narrow passage beneath the study that did not exist in the official records of the investigation. The brass key in Claire's possession bore the same unusual markings as a key described in the architect's private journal. According to the journal, it opened a door that had never been intended to appear on any building plan. Determined to discover what had been hidden, Claire visited the Ashford mansion on a cold, moonless evening. The front entrance had been sealed with heavy chains, but a side door stood slightly open, revealing a narrow hallway lined with portraits whose faces had faded into darkness. The air inside smelled of damp wood and old paper. Every footstep echoed through the empty rooms, stirring dust that had settled undisturbed for decades. At the end of the hallway, she found the study. The room looked almost exactly as it had been described in the original newspaper accounts. A massive desk stood beneath a cracked window, an antique clock rested on the mantelpiece, and the bookshelves covered the entire eastern wall. Behind them, Claire discovered a narrow outline in the wood, almost invisible beneath layers of grime. The brass key fit the lock perfectly. With a slow turn, the hidden door opened inward, revealing a cramped chamber containing a desk, several locked drawers, and a wall covered with photographs. The images showed Edward Ashford alongside members of his family, business associates, and people who had never been identified in the investigation. Red strings connected several photographs to handwritten notes, creating a web of relationships that suggested the murder had been connected to something far more complicated than a family dispute. On the desk lay a leather-bound ledger. Its pages contained financial records, private correspondence, and a list of payments made to people who had been interviewed during the original investigation. Beside several names were dates that matched the days those individuals had withdrawn their statements or left the town. The final page contained a single sentence written in Edward's handwriting. If you are reading this, the person who killed me is still protecting the secret. Beneath the sentence was a name. Claire recognized it immediately. It belonged to a prominent figure in Blackwood, someone who had attended Edward's funeral, spoken publicly about the tragedy, and helped persuade the authorities to close the case. Someone who was still alive. Before Claire could examine the ledger further, a sound came from the hallway outside the hidden chamber. Slow footsteps approached the study, followed by the unmistakable click of a lock turning. The door behind her slammed shut. She rushed toward it, but the handle would not move. Then she noticed a fresh envelope resting on the desk, one that had not been there when she entered the room. Inside was a photograph of Claire standing outside the mansion earlier that evening. On the back, a message had been written in black ink. Some crimes are forgotten for a reason. Stop looking, or you will become the next name in the ledger. Claire stared at the photograph, realizing that someone had been watching her long before she discovered the hidden room. The mansion had kept its secret for thirty years. Now that the truth was finally within reach, someone was willing to kill to keep it buried.
