Letters to Yesterday

— kenshi iwahashi —

Chapter One: The Letters I Never Sent

There are some things in life that we wish we could go back and change. Words we wish we had said, moments we wish we had held on to a little longer, and people we wish we had never let go of. For me, those things were all buried in yesterday. I used to believe that time could heal everything. That one day, the memories would stop hurting, the regrets would fade, and the people who left would eventually become nothing more than strangers in old photographs. But I was wrong. Because no matter how far we move forward, a part of us will always remain in the places we once called home. And sometimes, the past finds a way of reaching us, even when we spend years trying to forget it. It started with a letter. An ordinary envelope, yellowed at the edges, with my name written in handwriting I hadn't seen in years. There was no return address, no date, and no explanation for why it had suddenly appeared among the things I thought I had left behind. For a long time, I simply stared at it, afraid of what might be waiting inside. Because I knew that handwriting. I knew the person who had written my name so carefully, as if every letter mattered. The person who once promised they would never leave. The person whose absence had taught me how painful silence could be. I had spent so many years convincing myself that our story was over. But as I held that envelope in my trembling hands, I began to wonder if some stories were never really finished. Maybe the past wasn't asking me to return. Maybe it was asking me to remember. And perhaps, somewhere between the words I had never said and the goodbyes I had never understood, there was still a truth waiting to be found. I took a deep breath and opened the letter. I wasn't ready for what it said. But then again, I had never been ready to say goodbye to yesterday
